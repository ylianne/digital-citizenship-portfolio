MY LEARNING GOAL

Make better choices online.

I aim to pause before posting, check information before sharing it, protect personal details, communicate respectfully, and use digital and AI tools transparently. I want my online actions to reflect the same care, honesty, and professionalism that I value in face-to-face interactions.

02 — RESPONSIBLE ONLINE BEHAVIOR

Be thoughtful. Be respectful. Be aware.

Responsible digital participation includes how I communicate, what I leave behind, and how I protect information.

01

Netiquette

Netiquette is the practice of treating people respectfully when communicating online. It means using a suitable tone, reading a message before replying, avoiding insults and unnecessary all-caps text, staying on topic, and remembering that a real person will read the words I post. Disagreement is possible without being disrespectful.

02

Digital footprint

A digital footprint is the trail of information connected to my online activity. It can include posts, comments, reactions, uploaded photos, public profiles, and information others share about me. Some activity is intentional, while other details may be collected or shared without much thought. I should consider how a post may look later to classmates, instructors, employers, or the public.

03

Privacy

Privacy means thinking carefully about who can see my information and what they can do with it. I should avoid publishing sensitive details such as passwords, private contact information, account credentials, personal schedules, or someone else's information without permission. Privacy settings help, but they cannot guarantee that content will remain private after it is shared.

03 — THINK AND SIFT EVIDENCE

Pause before sending. Verify before sharing.

A message can be revised for kindness and clarity. A claim can be checked by tracing it to reliable evidence.

THINK · FICTIONAL CLASS CHAT EXAMPLE

Turning frustration into a constructive message

This is a realistic example created for the portfolio. It does not reproduce a real classmate's or instructor's message.

BEFORE
“This assignment is confusing and nobody explains anything. This is so annoying.”

The message communicates frustration, but it blames others and does not explain what help is needed.

AFTER
“I’m having trouble understanding the required format for this assignment. Could someone clarify the instructions or point me to the class announcement? Thank you.”

The revised message explains the difficulty, asks a clear question, and leaves room for a helpful response.

T — TrueIt states the actual issue: I do not understand the format.
H — HelpfulIt asks for clarification that can move the task forward.
I — InspiringIt models a calm approach and encourages cooperation.
N — NecessaryIt stays focused on information needed to complete the assignment.
K — KindIt avoids blame and uses a respectful request.
SIFT · PUBLIC, NON-SENSITIVE CLAIM

Can the Great Wall of China be seen from space with the naked eye?

This is a widely circulated claim and recurring online myth. The key is to distinguish unaided human vision from photographs taken with powerful camera lenses.

S

Stop

I paused before accepting or reposting the claim. The phrase “visible from space” is vague, so I needed to check whether it meant visible from low Earth orbit, from the Moon, or visible only in a zoomed photograph.

I

Investigate the source

I looked for a source with relevant expertise rather than relying on an unattributed meme. NASA's Johnson Space Center Earth observation FAQ directly addresses the claim and explains that the Great Wall is not visible to the naked eye from orbit and is not visible from the Moon.

NASA Johnson Space Center — Earth Observation FAQ ↗

F

Find better coverage

I compared the explanation with another space-agency source. The European Space Agency describes an astronaut's Great Wall image as a set of highly zoomed pictures taken from the International Space Station with a long camera lens. This supports the distinction between photographing the wall and seeing it unaided.

European Space Agency — Great Wall of China from space (May 28, 2021) ↗

T

Trace the claim to its original context

I checked NASA's explanation of the specific International Space Station photograph. NASA says the wall is difficult or impossible to see from Earth orbit without the high-powered lenses used for the photo and is not visible from the Moon. The image is photographic evidence made with equipment—not proof that a person can easily see the wall with unaided eyes.

NASA — Great Wall (April 2, 2009) ↗

MY CONCLUSION

The broad claim is misleading. The Great Wall has been photographed from orbit using powerful camera equipment, but that is different from seeing it with the naked eye. NASA also states that it is not visible from the Moon.

ARTIFACT 02 · BEFORE-A-DEADLINE MESSAGEAdapt to a real situation

Giving an early and honest update

Good day, Professor. I would like to inform you before the deadline that I am having difficulty completing the remaining part of the activity within the available time. I have completed [briefly state the completed part] and still need to finish [briefly state the remaining task]. I am working on it and would like to ask whether a short extension until [proposed date] would be possible. I understand that approval is subject to your instructions. Thank you for your consideration.

Replace the bracketed parts with accurate details. Do not invent a reason or disclose personal information that is not necessary.

ARTIFACT 03A · GROUP CHAT

Following up without blame

Hi! Just checking in about the part assigned to you. I understand everyone may have other responsibilities. Could you let us know when you might be available or whether you need help with anything? We want to make sure we can organize the remaining work before the deadline. Thank you!

ARTIFACT 03B · GROUP CHAT

Setting availability expectations

Hi, everyone! I am usually available to work on our project after [time] on [days]. I may not be able to reply immediately during class or other commitments, but I will check the group chat when I am available. Please send updates or questions here so I can catch up. Thank you!

WHY WAIT 48–72 HOURS BEFORE FOLLOWING UP?

For a non-urgent email, waiting 48 to 72 hours gives an instructor reasonable time to read and respond while managing classes, consultations, and other responsibilities. It also helps me avoid sending repeated messages too quickly. If a deadline is close or the matter is urgent, I should explain the time constraint politely and follow the instructor's stated communication procedure instead of automatically waiting.

05 — ACADEMIC HONESTY AND ETHICAL AI USE

Let the work remain mine.

Tools can support learning, but responsibility for accuracy, originality, and disclosure remains with the student.

PERSONAL INTEGRITY STATEMENT

To me, academic honesty means presenting work truthfully and taking responsibility for what I submit. It includes completing tasks with genuine effort, acknowledging ideas and materials that come from other people, and avoiding plagiarism or misrepresentation. I should not present copied work or AI-generated content as if I created every part independently. When I use a digital tool, I need to understand what it contributed, check the information it provides, and follow my instructor's rules about disclosure. I also believe that asking questions is better than pretending to understand something I do not. Academic integrity matters beyond school because technology professionals are expected to be trustworthy, careful with information, and accountable for their decisions.

CASE ANALYSIS · UNDISCLOSED AI-GENERATED ESSAY

What went wrong?

The student submitted AI-generated writing as if it were entirely their own and tried to disguise the source by changing only a few words. This misrepresents authorship and avoids responsibility for the ideas, accuracy, and sources in the essay. It may also violate the instructor's rules even if the AI output contains no direct quotation from another author.

What should the student have checked first?

The student should have checked the syllabus, assignment instructions, and course or institutional policy to learn whether AI use was allowed, limited, or prohibited and how it needed to be acknowledged. The student should also verify every factual statement and source rather than assuming an AI answer is correct.

How could AI have been used ethically?

Where permitted, AI could help explain a difficult concept, suggest research keywords, generate questions for brainstorming, or give feedback on clarity. The student should write the actual analysis, consult reliable sources, cite borrowed ideas, disclose AI assistance as required, and be able to explain and defend the submitted work.
